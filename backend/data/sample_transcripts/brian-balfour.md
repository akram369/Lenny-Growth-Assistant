# Brian Balfour on product-market fit, retention metrics, and compounding growth loops

## Transcript

Brian Balfour (00:02:10):
Most founders look at funnels when they should be looking at loops. Funnels are linear: you put more money in the top, some converts, and the rest falls out. To grow next month, you have to buy more at the top. Loops are circular: the output of one cycle becomes the input for the next cycle. That is how you get compounding growth rather than linear exhaustion.

Lenny (00:07:30):
Brian, how do you mathematically define true retention?

Brian Balfour (00:07:45):
Retention is the foundation of growth. If your retention curve does not flatten parallel to the x-axis, your product is a leaky bucket and any acquisition spending is lighting capital on fire. True retention is measured by natural frequency: if your product is a tax software, natural frequency is annual; if it's Slack, natural frequency is daily. You must measure cohort retention aligned with that natural frequency.

Lenny (00:18:40):
What is the Four Fits framework you created at Reforge?

Brian Balfour (00:19:00):
Product-Market Fit is only 25% of the puzzle. The Four Fits are: Market-Product Fit, Product-Channel Fit, Channel-Model Fit, and Model-Market Fit. Products are built to fit channels, not the other way around. If your price point is $10/year, you cannot use enterprise sales. If your product is $100k/year, virality won't close Fortune 500 deals. All four fits must be in equilibrium.
